# Philosophy

## Stop first, teach second

Prompt Sensei does two jobs in order. First it protects your usage: the expensive mistakes are made before the prompt runs. Then it teaches: a rewrite plus one habit makes the next prompt better without a lecture.

## Stage matters more than polish

A rough question is the right prompt when you are still exploring. A short "ok do it" is the right prompt when the plan is settled. Judging both like a big build request is wrong and discouraging, so Prompt Sensei classifies the stage first and only asks for what that stage needs.

## Kind, specific, one thing

- Never "bad prompt". Say what it is good for now, then the next step.
- One habit at a time. People change one habit, not seven.
- Specific beats general: "add what you expected and what happened" beats "be clearer".

## Fit beats generic

The best rewrite is shaped by what you already have: your project instructions, your trackers, your naming rules, your earlier decisions. A generic "better prompt" that ignores your system makes a mess. A rewrite that reuses it makes the work fit.

## Your call wins

It pauses at most once per task, offers a choice, and runs whatever you pick. It is advice, not a gate.

## Private by default

No files written, no history kept, nothing sent anywhere. It reads only what is already in the session.
